# Third-order source construction — November 23, 2026

## Change

A third-order target now gets a bounded source-planning pass before drafting: two exact original-source rules must join through the same intermediate under the assigned objective. The plan remains untrusted writer input; neither the blind solver nor the source reviewer receives its proposed intermediate or key. The final question still needs independently grounded reasoning, a unique independently matched answer, objective ownership, and reviewed connection IDs.

The writer receives both target and supporting connections, including an already-practiced prerequisite when needed for integration. A focused plan-first prompt replaces conflicting general application scaffolds for wholly third-order batches. School examples retain their existing style-selection path; lecture evidence remains the factual authority, and supplemental review contexts still attach normally. Mixed and second-order batches keep their existing paths with the integration contract added where appropriate.

Reviewers share the same definition: interpret the observation, apply rule 1 to infer the hidden intermediate, then apply rule 2 to predict the endpoint. Two dependent relationships suffice; three relationships are not required. Rules already supplied in the stem cannot count as inferences. Named pathways cannot automatically collapse two genuinely necessary links into one. Parallel classifications, answer-choice shortcuts, unsupported clinical additions, and ambiguous keys remain ineligible.

Flattened PDF exports now retain explicit ascending standalone page headers. Connection-map retrieval skips repeated objective outlines and prioritizes relevant original factual passages. Exact original quotations needed by the target/plan are pinned into the independent source catalog; proposed solutions are not. Multiple current-source reviewed map connections permit an integration attempt even when the objective verb is "Describe"; this never grants item approval.

Older saved third-order questions require re-review under the hidden-two-link policy before entering a new set. Historical answers, scores, and evidence are not rewritten. Second-order reuse is unchanged.

A successful single-item run does not establish reliable 20-item completion, universal third-order yield across lectures, or learner mastery. The two-item shortfall remains explicit; gates were not bypassed to fill it. Live model timing includes planning, drafting, review and any repair, and can vary with provider availability.

Additional narrow cross-lecture probes: NB34 NSCI.0175 (axonal-terminal/cleft/end-plate lesions) and NB43 NSCI.0250 (horizontal vestibulo-ocular circuitry) each retained 0/1 after two bounded replacement rounds (66.0 and 51.7 seconds). Those candidates remained simple syndrome-to-mechanism/response lookups or supplied their first link, and some repairs introduced unsupported findings or neighboring tasks. They are explicit shortfalls, not evidence of third-order success across those lectures. The code permits supported integration and rejects unsupported depth; it does not guarantee that every narrow objective can fill a requested integration set from the available text.

## Release routing and final regression

Advanced integration planning, drafting, blind solving, repair and review now request the configured escalation tier when advertised (Kimi in the audited bridge), while preserving the existing capacity fallbacks and configured cloud limits. Other requests retain their normal quality route. Tests cover an unavailable escalation tier and escalation-to-quality fallback; no additional service or credentials were introduced.

The final automatic-route NB37 check retained 1/1 in 46.1 seconds. The hidden sequence was cortical origin → ipsilateral pontine relay → crossed cerebellar destination through the middle peduncle, with same-category destination/peduncle choices and the new hidden-two-link policy stamp. Manual review confirmed that the stem did not provide either projection rule. A separate final NMJ probe retained 0/1 in 49.9 seconds; cross-lecture yield remains a limitation.

An additional observed false-positive endpoint (motor-cortex lesion → body side) could bypass the claimed cerebellar route through direct motor laterality. The answer-route gate now rejects that narrow third-order shortcut, with a paired regression proving a genuine cerebellar-destination task remains eligible. A model's claimed extra steps cannot bypass this screen.
